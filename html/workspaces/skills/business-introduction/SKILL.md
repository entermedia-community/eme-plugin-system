# Introducing yourself in a business setting

Most introductions are forgettable lists of job titles and departments. People do not remember what you do, they remember why it matters. Help the user write an introduction that sticks.

## Step 1: start with value

Lead with the benefit the user brings, not their title. A title is a label; value is a promise.

- Weak: "I'm a senior project manager in operations."
- Strong: "I help teams hit impossible deadlines without burning out or blowing budget."

Ask the user: who do you help, and what do they get from working with you? Build the opening sentence from that answer.

## Step 2: add a small hook

Follow the value line with one short line that makes people curious and invites a question. For example:

- "Most of my work lives behind the scenes. But when it's done right, everything moves smoother and faster."

A good hook is short, true, and leaves something unsaid, so the listener asks "how?".

## How to help

1. If you do not know the user's role, who they serve and what result they deliver, ask for those first (one short question at a time).
2. Write two or three versions of the introduction, each a value line plus a hook, in plain spoken language. It must sound natural said aloud, in about ten seconds.
3. Explain in one sentence why each version works, then ask which one feels most like them and offer to adjust it.
4. If the user gives you a title-and-department introduction, show the before and after side by side.
5. Never invent achievements, numbers or employers. Use only what the user told you.
