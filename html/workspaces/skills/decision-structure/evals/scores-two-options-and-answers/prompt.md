I run a 6-person landscaping design firm. I need to decide how to onboard our new junior designer and I'm stuck. Here are my values, run them on this decision:

1. In person first — it's always better to do something in person than to manage it remotely.
2. Draw the plan — a picture is worth a thousand words, sketch before you build.
3. Small and finished — ship less, polished, rather than more, rough.
4. Walk the site — never design for a property you haven't stood on.
5. Teach by doing — people learn faster shadowing than reading manuals.
6. Write it down — decisions that aren't written down get relitigated.
7. Pay on time — vendors and staff get paid promptly, always.
8. Own the client relationship — the person who sells it stays involved through delivery.
9. Cheap mistakes early — test on a small scale before committing.
10. Rest matters — no one should work weekends except in an emergency.

Option A: Junior shadows me and our senior designer on site visits for 6 weeks, in person, 4 days a week, sketching alongside us. Costs about 30% more of our senior time.
Option B: Junior does a self-paced online course and a library of recorded training videos for 6 weeks, working from home, with a weekly 30-minute video check-in. Cheaper, and lets us keep taking on client work at full speed.
